Overlap exists to stop a sentence that answers the question from being cut in half. That is the whole of the justification, and it is worth remembering, because every token you overlap is a token you store twice, embed twice, and search twice.

Note — the cost is not linear in the overlap fraction. At 25 percent overlap you store a third more chunks, not a quarter more, because the last window of every document is padded.

A windowing function is four lines and one guard. The guard is the part people leave out, and it is the part that turns a bad configuration into an infinite loop rather than a warning.

def chunks(text, size=512, overlap=64): """Yield token windows with a fixed overlap.""" tokens = tokenizer.encode(text) step = size - overlap if step <= 0: raise ValueError("overlap must be smaller than size") for start in range(0, len(tokens), step): window = tokens[start:start + size] if len(window) < overlap: break

The final guard on line 9 drops a trailing window shorter than the overlap itself. Without it the last chunk of every document is a near-duplicate of the one before it, and your index grows by the number of documents for no recall at all — see Figure 4.4.

Careful — tokenizers are not interchangeable here. A 512-token window under one tokenizer is not a 512-token window under another, and re-indexing after a tokenizer change is not optional.

Two vectors can be close in the sense that matters to a metric and far apart in the sense that matters to a reader. Most of the disappointment people report with vector search comes from this gap, not from the index.

DEFINITION · FIRST USE
Cosine similarity — the cosine of the angle between two vectors, ignoring their magnitudes. Two documents on the same topic score alike whether one is a sentence or a chapter.
