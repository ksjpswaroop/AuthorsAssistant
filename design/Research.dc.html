WHAT THE SOURCES SAY

Nothing in the retrieved literature measures recall at book length. Every result on the shortlist evaluates passages drawn from Wikipedia-scale documents, where a chunk is a fraction of the document rather than a fraction of a chapter.

That is the gap this paper can claim, and it is narrower than the current draft of §1 asserts — two of the twelve sources come close.

Recall is almost always reported as top-k retrieval accuracy over a fixed passage length — 100 words in Karpukhin 2020, inherited without restatement by most of what follows.

No source on this list reports recall as a function of chunk length. If §3 wants that curve, it has to be measured here.

Published chunk sizes cluster at 100 words and at 512 tokens, and the choice is nearly always justified by the encoder's input limit rather than by any measurement.

Izacard 2021 is the exception worth citing: it scales passage count instead of passage size.
Thin evidence — two sources, neither direct

Nothing retrieved isolates overlap as a variable. The nearest arguments are indirect: late interaction in Khattab 2020 reduces what a boundary costs, and length normalisation in Robertson 2009 predicts overlap should inflate scores without improving ranking.

Treat this as an open question in §6, not a settled result.

Still reading. On the evidence so far, ranking degrades before recall does: the right chunk stays in the candidate set and falls out of the top-k.

Malkov 2020 is filed here only to rule out the index as the cause.
